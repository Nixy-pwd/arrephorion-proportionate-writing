# Necessary vs decorative caveats

Every example below is quoted from the B1 outputs ([b1-outputs.md](b1-outputs.md)). No example is invented. Each one is classified by whether it changes a judgment the reader would otherwise make.

## Necessary: changes what the reader concludes

**States what an inconclusive result does not license, in both directions** (E, Treatment):

> consequently, this criterion provides neither evidence of transportability nor evidence of a violation.

The reader would otherwise be tempted to count C2 toward the failure.

**States a provisional status once, with its consequence** (E, Treatment):

> Because Phase 3B parser and measurement-instrument validation remains outstanding, this verdict retains the official status PROVISIONAL_PENDING_PHASE_3B; accordingly, claims of definitive transportability failure should be reserved until that validation has been completed.

This tells the reader what not to claim yet, not just that uncertainty exists.

**Scopes a conclusion to the readers it affects** (C, Treatment):

> This finding therefore argues against treating the method as fully transportable to the new domain, particularly for applications that depend on accurate cross-staff pitch relationships.

## Decorative: repeats something the reader already has

**The same limitation, twice in a row** (B, Control):

> Nevertheless, this conclusion remains PROVISIONAL_PENDING_PHASE_3B, as parser and measurement-instrument validation have not yet been performed. The present verdict should therefore be interpreted as a provisional determination under the current analytical and measurement framework, pending Phase 3B validation before a definitive conclusion regarding domain transportability is drawn.

The second sentence adds no new judgment.

**Stacked hedges on one claim** (F, Control):

> cannot yet be considered reliably transportable to the new domain in its entirety

"Yet", "reliably" and "in its entirety" each soften the same failed verdict.

## Negative: hedging that changes the result

**Softening a formal verdict** (D, Control):

> Until that validation is performed, the findings support a provisional conclusion of limited domain transportability, with particular concern regarding cross-staff pitch interval preservation, rather than an unconditional rejection of the method's applicability to the new domain.

The formal verdict was DOMAIN_TRANSPORTABILITY_FAIL. Recasting it as "limited transportability" is not caution. It moves the conclusion.

## Missing: a required caveat nobody wrote

C3's lower bound (≈3.0795) exceeded its margin (3) by about 2.65%. The domain owner had pre-coded this as a caveat the text must include, because a reader judging how robust the failure is needs it. Leaving it out does not make any sentence false, so in the skill's terms it is Weighed: the author's choice, which this author made in advance. None of the six outputs, in either group, stated it. A rule that only removes caveats cannot supply one the input leaves implicit. Step 1 of the skill, which computes what the source implies, exists because of this case.
