1\. The gradual character of identity. Paragraph 21 emphasizes that relations and transitions diminish by insensible degrees. ## 2. Discussion paragraph variants

### Variant A — Direct

Floor caveats only

Hume rejects a simple, enduring self: no constant, invariable impression could ground its idea, and introspection discloses only particular perceptions. He therefore describes the mind as a succession or bundle, not a theatre-like container; only perceptions constitute it. This argument concerns personal identity as thought or imagination, not as passion or self-concern, and Hume allows that others may experience a simple, continued self. Identity is attributed through relations and ease of transition, but no standard fixes its boundaries; memory chiefly discovers causal continuity, rather than producing identity, and extends it beyond recollection. The Appendix does not withdraw the case against a simple self, but Hume cannot explain the connection among distinct perceptions, which he treats as distinct existences without any real connection perceived by the mind. He calls the difficulty beyond his present understanding, not absolutely insoluble.&#x20;
